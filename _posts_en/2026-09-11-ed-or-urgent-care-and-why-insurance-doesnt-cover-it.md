"I need to see a doctor urgently and I have health insurance — can I go to a private hospital?"

Most people have never thought about this until the moment they need to, and then it is the first question they ask. Worth knowing before you need it.

## The common misunderstanding

I hear this a lot, from parents especially: healthcare here is slow, even a GP appointment can be a week away, so if my child spikes a fever at midnight or something urgent happens, what do I do? I had better buy insurance.

**That reasoning does not hold, because health insurance in New Zealand does not cover emergencies.** Which sounds like a bad deal until you see how the system is arranged.

## How the system splits the work

**The ordinary pathway**: book a GP appointment, then get referred on to a specialist, diagnostics and so on.

**The emergency pathway**: go straight to the emergency department at a public hospital. No appointment needed, but you will wait — and the queue is not first-come, first-served. You are seen in order of clinical severity.

So in New Zealand, **emergencies belong to the public hospital system**. Health insurance covers the costs on the non-urgent pathway.

And you do not need to worry that public emergency care is slow in the way that elective care is. **Public hospital emergency departments in New Zealand do this well.** If your situation is critical and life is at risk, you will be treated quickly.

That is why I keep saying: living in New Zealand, with the public system for emergencies and your insurance set up properly for everything else, healthcare is genuinely one of the things you do not need to worry much about.

## Urgent care clinics

Besides hospital EDs there are urgent care clinics — Shorecare, White Cross and others — which you can also walk into without an appointment.

Note that these clinics **charge a consultation fee**, similar to seeing a GP. Public hospital ED is free if you are eligible: a work visa of two years or more, permanent residence, or citizenship.

## So which one do you go to?

**Go to a hospital ED when the situation is genuinely urgent** and delaying treatment would put life at risk. You will generally not queue — you will be assessed and treated straight away.

**Go to an urgent care clinic when you need to be seen soon but it is not that urgent.** The ED wait in that situation can be long, and an urgent care clinic will usually get you in front of a doctor faster.

### Two examples

**Chest pain — go to ED.** Cardiac symptoms are urgent, so at a hospital you will generally not wait long and will be prioritised for assessment.

**A sudden fever — urgent care is reasonable.** Turn up at ED with this and you may well be told it is a four or five hour wait. At an urgent care clinic you might wait two hours, see a doctor, and pay the consultation fee yourself.

## They refer between each other

In principle you can take anything to a hospital ED, but if it is not urgent your wait will be far longer than at an urgent care clinic.

The two also refer in both directions. When a hospital is overloaded, less serious patients may be directed or sent on to an urgent care clinic. And patients initially treated at an urgent care clinic are sometimes transferred by ambulance to hospital for further observation and investigation.

## What urgent care costs

Fees vary by clinic, by the patient's age, and by time of day. There are many urgent care clinics around the country and none of them charge quite the same, so it is worth looking up the one nearest you before you need it.
