The sentence I hear most often when I review someone's policy is: "I had no idea that was in there."

Insurance is not only for the year something terrible happens. Every wording carries a set of benefits you can use without a major claim — nobody just tells you about them.

So here they are in one place: nine categories, all checked against the August 2026 wordings. One caveat first — not every product and not every option includes these, so go by the version of your own policy before relying on any of it.

## 1. Wisdom teeth come out of health insurance, not dental cover

In New Zealand, removing wisdom teeth counts as minor surgery and is covered by health insurance in its own right.

The conditions: your dentist or doctor has to recommend removal, your policy must not carry a relevant exclusion, the waiting periods differ by insurer, and the work usually has to be done by an oral surgeon.

## 2. Health check allowances after a few years in force

**AIA**: after three years in force, $500 every three years towards screening you choose. The wording lists bone density, bowel, breast, cervical and heart screening among the options.

**nib**: under the Proactive Health Option, after two continuous years there is a Loyalty – Health Check Benefit covering a check-up with a GP or nurse.

**Southern Cross**: a $50 "being active" allowance each year after three years in force.

If you have gone a whole year without being sick and feel like the premiums were wasted, this is the easiest way to get some of it back.

## 3. Sterilisation procedures

**AIA**: up to $5,000, with prior approval required.

**Partners Life**: after two continuous years, sterilisation for contraceptive purposes is covered outright, **with no excess**. Reversal is not covered.

**nib**: a Loyalty – Sterilisation Benefit, once per person.

## 4. Money for staying in a public hospital

Two Partners Life benefits are worth knowing about here. Both are **optional benefits** in the wording, so you only have them if they were selected and paid for — check your Policy Schedule.

**Public Hospital Cash Benefit**: for a stay in a public hospital, $300 a night from the third consecutive night onwards, up to 10 nights. The wording's own example: a five-night stay pays $300 each for nights four and five, $600 in total.

**Public Hospital Credit Benefit**: if you could have gone private and claimed for surgery, non-surgical treatment or major diagnostics, and you chose the public system instead and were admitted for more than 24 hours, Partners Life **refunds the health insurance premiums paid for that life assured over the previous 12 months**. Using the public system costs you nothing.

## 5. Rehabilitation costs after you are discharged

**Partners Life** has a Recovery Support Benefit, again optional. Within six months of discharge from a private hospital or day surgery facility, it covers rehabilitation consultations and treatment recommended by your treating specialist, at $500 per admission.

**nib** includes post-discharge physiotherapy and home nursing in its base cover.

## 6. When money is tight, premiums can be paused

Partners Life has a Premium Holiday, available for redundancy, bankruptcy, or leaving work to care for someone who is ill, for up to six months. nib has Suspending your Cover: after a year in force you can suspend for unemployment, going overseas, or parental leave.

This is a far better option than cancelling, for [reasons I have written about here](/en/blog/does-life-insurance-pay-anything-back-nz/).

## 7. Increasing your cover for life events, without underwriting

Buying a house, increasing the mortgage, a pay rise, getting married, having a baby — these are all moments when your sum insured should go up. But if you apply again now, is your health as good as it was when you first took out the policy?

**Chubb** has a Life Events Benefit: when one of the listed life events happens, you can increase your life cover with **no health assessment**. The cap is the lesser of the mortgage increase, five times the salary increase, or $250,000, with the total not exceeding 75% of the originally underwritten sum insured, applied for within 180 days of the event. Trauma or total permanent disability cover can be added alongside it under the Life Events Additional Cover Benefit.

**AIA** trauma cover has a Future Insurability Benefit and a Special Events Increase Facility that work the same way, with a six-month stand-down on the increase.

## 8. Children are covered by their parents' trauma policy

If either parent holds trauma cover, the children usually have built-in children's trauma cover already and do not need their own.

**AIA**: a Built-in Children's Trauma Benefit paying the lesser of $50,000 or 50% of the parent's sum insured. Newborns have a separate Built-in Newborn Children's Benefit with a 12-month stand-down.

**Partners Life**: the Child's Trauma Cover Benefit pays on top of the parent's sum insured. Before the child turns 21 it can also be converted into their own standalone trauma policy of up to $50,000, **with no health assessment**.

This is part of why I keep saying that [children do not need large trauma sums insured — protect the adults first](/en/blog/who-to-insure-first-in-your-family/).

## 9. Extra money that comes after a claim is paid

**Chubb life cover**: the Bereavement Support Benefit advances up to $25,000 immediately for urgent costs such as the funeral. The Funeral Transfer Benefit covers repatriation of the body to New Zealand or to the country of origin. The Financial and Legal Advice Benefit reimburses up to $2,500 of financial or legal advice to help the family plan around the payout.

**AIA trauma cover**: within three months of a full claim being paid, up to $2,500 of financial or legal advice is reimbursed.
