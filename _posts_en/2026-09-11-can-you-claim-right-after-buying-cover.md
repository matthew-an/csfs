Can I use my policy as soon as I have bought it? If I end up seeing a doctor shortly after taking out cover, will the claim be declined? I have heard the phrase "waiting period" somewhere — do I have to get past it before anything is covered?

The answer depends on which cover you are asking about, and the three work quite differently.

## Health insurance: generally no waiting period

**For standard health insurance there is no general waiting period.** Apart from some insurers applying a one-year wait to wisdom teeth, other conditions have no stand-down at all. Once the policy is in force, medical costs that arise can be claimed.

### The condition attached to that

You must have disclosed your health honestly when you applied — including symptoms you have not yet seen anyone about.

### Claiming soon after taking out cover

If you claim shortly after the policy starts, the insurer will assess it more carefully. If they find the condition being claimed relates to a pre-existing one that was not disclosed, the claim can be declined. **But if it genuinely arose after cover started, then even if that was very soon afterwards, you have nothing to worry about.**

This is the thing to understand about how New Zealand insurers manage risk: they use pre-existing condition exclusions, not a blanket six-month wait. Which is also why [how you disclose your history matters so much](/en/blog/buying-cover-with-a-pre-existing-condition/).

## Trauma cover: some conditions do have a stand-down

**For trauma cover, some conditions carry a genuine waiting period.** Cancer is the usual example, with a three-month stand-down — a cancer diagnosis within three months of the policy starting is not paid. Heart attack and stroke generally work the same way.

## Income protection: the phrase means something different

With income protection, the "waiting period" is something you choose yourself when you apply. Once you have been unable to do your own job for longer than that period, the insurer starts paying the agreed monthly benefit.

It is not a restriction placed on you — it is a setting, and one of the two biggest levers on the premium. There is more on how to choose it in [income protection vs trauma cover](/en/blog/income-protection-your-financial-safety-net/).

## In short

| Cover | What "waiting period" means |
|---|---|
| Health insurance | Generally none. Wisdom teeth are the common exception, at some insurers |
| Trauma cover | A 90-day stand-down on major conditions such as cancer, heart attack and stroke |
| Income protection | The elimination period you chose, commonly 4 weeks to 2 years, before monthly payments start |
